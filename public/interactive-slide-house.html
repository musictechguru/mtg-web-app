The History of House Music

"House music is a feeling." Born in Chicago's underground clubs from the ashes of disco, House revolutionized dance music through the Roland TR-909 drum machine, Korg M1 piano and organ stabs, gospel vocal hooks, and tape-spliced extended 12-inch club mixes.

🎧 Classroom Slide 2 • The Landmark Blueprint

A House Classic: Black Box & "Ride On Time" (1989)

Released in 1989 by Italian House group Black Box, "Ride On Time" exemplifies every defining sonic and technological element of early commercial house music.

Anatomy of a House Smash

  • Tempo: 120–130 BPM with a steady four-on-the-floor kick drum.
  • Disco & Soul Influences: Deep roots in 1970s Philadelphia soul and upbeat Euro-disco.
  • Synthesizers & Samplers: Korg M1, Akai S900 digital sampler, and Roland TR-909.
  • Vocal Sampling: Sampled Loleatta Holloway's 1980 disco anthem "Love Sensation" into the Akai S900.
  • Featured Piano: Bright, syncopated chord stabs cutting through the mix.
  • Club Mixing: Extended beat intro and outro engineered specifically for DJs to beatmatch.

The 12-Inch Single Format

House music was created for and distributed on 12-inch vinyl singles. Wider groove spacing allowed cutting engineers to maximize bass frequencies and dynamic range without needle skipping.

⚡ Classroom Slides 3 & 4 • 1977–1979

Roots in Disco, "I Feel Love" & Demolition Disco

Electronic dance music did not begin in a vacuum. It was born when futuristic synthesizer production met the underground resistance of post-disco America.

Donna Summer & Giorgio Moroder (1977)

"I Feel Love" (1977): Produced by Giorgio Moroder in Munich and recorded on 16-track tape. Moroder used a Moog Modular sequencer to drive an unyielding 16th-note bassline and generated white noise from the Moog for hi-hats. Remarkably, the kick drum is the only instrument played live on acoustic drums!

Demolition Disco (1979)

On July 12, 1979, the anti-disco backlash reached a fever pitch at Chicago's Comiskey Park with Disco Demolition Night. Major record labels dropped disco artists, forcing the music underground.

The Bedroom Producer Revolution: Dance music moved out of expensive commercial recording studios and into the homes of young producers. Instead of hiring live string sections and session drummers, they turned to affordable drum machines and synthesizers.

👑 Classroom Slide 5 • The Godfather of House

Frankie Knuckles: The Warehouse & "Your Love"

From New York disco roots to the birthplace of House music at 206 South Jefferson Street, Chicago.

Frankie Knuckles at The Warehouse

Knuckles began as a NYC disco DJ alongside Larry Levan. In 1977, he took up residency at The Warehouse (and later his own club, the Power Plant). He took classic Philly soul and Euro-disco records, spliced reel-to-reel tape to create extended instrumental breakdowns, and mixed in Roland TR-909 drum machine beats live during his sets.

"Your Love" (1987) with Jamie Principle

Collaborating with singer/songwriter Jamie Principle, Knuckles produced "Your Love". Built on a pulsating, hypnotic 16th-note synthesizer arpeggio and sensual, whispered vocals over a punchy drum machine beat, it became the eternal anthem of deep, emotional Chicago house.

🎹 Classroom Slide 6 • The House Music Anthem

Marshall Jefferson: Piano House & Deep House

Marshall Jefferson fundamentally transformed house music from minimalist drum tracks into emotive, song-driven anthems with syncopated piano chords.

"Move Your Body" (1986)

Subtitled "The House Music Anthem", this track introduced acoustic piano to house music. Jefferson famously recorded the piano chords at a slow 40 BPM and then sped the tape up to 120 BPM! This created a razor-sharp, percussive piano attack that cut through club sound systems with tremendous clarity.

Pioneering "Deep House"

Beyond high-energy club anthems, Jefferson pioneered Deep House—infusing house music with jazz chords, electric piano Rhodes voicings, and lush string pads (e.g., CeCe Rogers "Someday").

Key Specification Fact: Marshall Jefferson DJed at both The Warehouse and Power Plant, shaping the emotional, melodic identity of Chicago dance culture.
🧪 Classroom Slide 7 • The TB-303 Revolution

Phuture: Acid House & The TB-303 Resonance

A radical, minimalist branch of house emerged in Chicago when young producers discovered the alien squelch of an analog bassline synthesizer.

Phuture: "Acid Tracks" (1987)

Chicago group Phuture (DJ Pierre, Spanky, Herb J) acquired a second-hand Roland TB-303. By running a repetitive 16-step bassline and manually twisting the Cutoff Frequency while driving the Resonance into extreme self-oscillation, DJ Pierre created the screaming, squelchy timbre of Acid House.

Second Summer of Love (1988–1989)

Exported on Trax Records TX142, "Acid Tracks" became an anthem for the UK's Second Summer of Love. It moved from London clubs (Shoom) and Manchester (The Haçienda) into massive orbital warehouse raves, establishing house music as the dominant UK youth culture.

🏆 Classroom Slide 8 • Chicago Trailblazers

Honourable Mentions: Saunders, Farley & Joe Smooth

The essential Chicago pioneers highlighted in the classroom slide deck who built the infrastructure of house music.

Jesse Saunders

Released "On and On" (1984), officially recognized as the first Chicago House record. Created with a Roland TR-808, Korg Poly-61, and tape splices on Jes Say Records.

Farley 'Jackmaster' Funk

Collaborated with Darryl Pandy on "Love Can't Turn Around" (1986), which reached #10 on the UK charts and introduced flamboyant Chicago house performance to Top of the Pops.

Joe Smooth

Produced "Promised Land" (1987) on DJ International. Blended uplifting soul vocals, major 7th piano chords, and civil rights optimism into a timeless worldwide anthem.

🌟 Classroom Slide 10 • 1991–1993 Chart Domination

Onwards into the 1990s: Masters At Work, Waters & Robin S

In the early 1990s, house music matured into a sophisticated, multi-million dollar global recording industry centered on iconic vocal hooks and digital presets.

Masters at Work (1991)

"The Ha Dance": Pioneered intricate vocal slicing and stuttered percussion that became the foundational rhythm of NYC ballroom and vogue culture.

Crystal Waters (1991)

"Gypsy Woman (She's Homeless)": Written by Waters and produced by the Basement Boys, built around a haunting organ chord vamp and iconic vocal refrain.

🚀 Classroom Slide 11 • 1995–1999 Remix Culture & Subgenres

The Late 1990s: Todd Terry, St Germain, Garage & Hard House

As highlighted in Slide 11 of the presentation, the late 1990s saw house fragment into distinctive, powerful subgenres.

Everything But The Girl (1995)

"Missing" (Todd Terry Remix): Todd Terry revolutionized pop remixing, setting Tracey Thorn's folk ballad over swung 4/4 drums and bouncy bass.

St Germain (1995)

"Alabama Blues" (Todd Edwards Remix): Ludovic Navarre combined deep blues sampling with Todd Edwards' complex vocal micro-chopping. Jazzy and soulful.

Roy Davis Jr. (1996)

"Gabriel" (ft. Peven Everett): "Garage is here." Featuring live trumpet and soulful vocals, this track became the blueprint for UK Garage and 2-step.

🇫🇷 Classroom Slide 12 • French Filter Touch

Daft Punk: Masters of the Sampler

As highlighted in Slide 12 of the classroom deck, Daft Punk delved deep into 1970s and 1980s disco records, turning micro-samples into massive 21st-century anthems.

Sampling Science on Discovery (2001)

Daft Punk took obscure disco and funk vinyl records, sampled 1-bar or 2-beat fragments into E-mu and Ensoniq hardware samplers, and rearranged them into completely new musical themes (e.g. sampling Edwin Birdsong's "Cola Bottle Baby" for "Harder Better Faster Stronger").

Filter Sweeps & Sidechain Pumping

  • Low-Pass Filter Automation: Sweeping resonant low-pass filters (Mutronics Mutator) to build tension before beat drops.
  • Alesis 3630 Compressor: Routing the heavy TR-909 kick into the compressor's sidechain detector to aggressively pump the disco sample in time with the groove.
  • Phasers & Flangers: Adding thick 8-stage analog phasing across the master drum bus.
🎛️ Classroom Slides 13 & 16 • Hardware Archive

The Tech: Drum Machines & Samplers

The core hardware units specified in Slides 13 and 16 of the presentation that provided the rhythmic backbone of House.

Slide 13: Drum Machines

The Roland Rhythm Composers

  • Roland TR-808 (1980): Analog bridged-T network kick drum producing deep 50 Hz sub-bass booms (used on Jesse Saunders' "On and On").
  • Roland TR-909 (1983): The absolute king of House music. Analog kick, snare, and toms paired with 6-bit digital PCM cymbals and offbeat open hi-hats.
  • Roland TR-707 (1985): All-digital PCM drum machine used extensively in early Chicago acid tracks for snappy snares.
  • Roland TR-606 (1981): Compact companion to the TB-303, known for its metallic, cutting analog hats.
Slide 16: Samplers

Digital Samplers

  • Akai S900 / S1000: The industry workhorses (12-bit and 16-bit). Used by Black Box, Todd Terry, and Robin S to chop vocal stabs and disco piano loops.
  • E-mu SP-12 / SP-1200: 12-bit / 26 kHz crunchy sampling with ringing aliasing filters, beloved for drum hits.
  • Casio FZ-10: Affordable 16-bit rackmount sampler used by UK bedroom producers.
  • Akai MPC (60 / 3000): Roger Linn's 16-velocity pad workflow providing legendary unquantized swing.
🎹 Classroom Slides 14 & 15 • Synthesis Archive

The Tech: Synthesizers (Analog to Digital PCM)

The synthesizers detailed in Slides 14 and 15 that defined basslines, chords, and leads across the history of House.

Slide 14: Analog Synths

Analog Classics

  • Roland TB-303: Monophonic bass synthesizer with 18 dB/oct resonant low-pass filter and internal 16-step sequencer with slides/accents.
  • Roland MC-202 (1983): MicroComposer synthesizer with SH-101 voice circuitry and dual-track sequencer.
  • Korg Poly-61 (1982): Digitally controlled analog polyphonic synth used on Jesse Saunders' "On and On".
  • Roland Juno-60 / Juno-106: Rich analog DCO poly-synth with built-in stereo chorus, famous for lush house pads.
Slide 15: Digital Workstations

Digital & Lead Synths

  • Korg M1 (1988): The bestselling digital workstation of all time. Provided the iconic "Piano 1" house chord stabs and the legendary "Organ 2" bassline.
  • Roland SH-101: Fast, punchy monophonic analog bass synth with snappy envelopes.
  • Yamaha DX7: FM digital frequency modulation synth behind punchy solid basslines.
  • Korg MS-20: Semi-modular dual-filter analog synth with aggressive screaming character.
💾 Classroom Slides 17 & 18 • Recording & Processing

The Tech: Atari ST, Fostex R8 & Effects Units

How bedroom and indie studios actually recorded, sequenced, and processed house music tracks in the 1980s and 1990s.

Slide 17: Sequencers & Tape

Atari ST & Fostex R8 Multitrack

  • Atari ST Computer: The centerpiece of 1980s/90s dance studios because it had built-in MIDI IN/OUT ports. Ran Steinberg Pro24, Cubase, and C-Lab Notator with ultra-tight MIDI clock timing.
  • Fostex R8 8-Track Tape Recorder: Affordable 1/4-inch reel-to-reel recorder allowing bedroom producers to record vocals and live instruments.
  • SMPTE to MTC Converter: Stripped a SMPTE timecode track onto audio track 8 of the tape to slave the Atari MIDI sequencer to tape playback!
Slide 18: Outboard Effects Units

Essential Hardware Processors

  • Alesis MIDIverb II: 16-bit digital reverb providing preset 29 (warm bloom) and reverse reverbs for vocal stabs.
  • Yamaha SPX90: Legendary multi-effects unit providing "Symphonic" chorus and pitch-shifted thickening.
  • Ensoniq DP4: 4-channel parallel processor used by Daft Punk for lush phasers and filter modulation.
  • ProCo RAT Distortion: Heavy analog guitar distortion stompbox used to overdrive drum machines and TB-303 basslines.
📋 Classroom Slide 19 • Definitive Specification Checklist

The Characteristics of House Music (Slide 19)

The complete, definitive 18-point stylistic checklist straight from Slide 19 of the presentation.

Rhythm & Tempo

1. Repetitive Beats: Strict 4-to-the-floor kick.
2. 120–130 BPM: Standard dance club tempo.
3. Distinctive Hi-Hat Patterns: Offbeat open hats.
4. Swing Quantise: 57–62% shuffle swing on 16ths.
5. Drum Samples & Breakbeats: Sliced funk fills.
6. Made for DJs: Extended 32-bar mix intros.

Harmony & Instruments

7. Disco & Soul Influences: Minor 7th/9th chords.
8. Piano Riffs: Syncopated triplet chord stabs.
9. Organ Bassline: Korg M1 Organ 2 preset.
10. Heavy Basslines: Deep sub-bass energy.
11. The TB-303 Squelch: Resonant filter sweeps.
12. Lush Strings & Pads: Deep House textures.

Production & Culture

13. Vocal Samples: Looped phrases and belts.
14. Instrumental Samples: Brass and chord stabs.
15. Drum Machines: TR-808, TR-909, TR-707.
16. Groove Based Music: Unrelenting kinetic drive.
17. Spawned Subgenres: Garage, Hard, Deep.
18. Club Subculture: Warehouse parties & raves.

Section B Matrix: 1980s Bedroom DIY vs 1990s Commercial Club

How technological changes between the 1980s and 1990s transformed house music for your 10-mark comparison essay.

Feature 1980s Chicago DIY (Saunders, Phuture, Trax) 1990s Commercial Club (Robin S, Peniston, Black Box)
Sequencing & MIDI Standalone hardware sequencers (TR-909 internal step keys, TB-303, DIN Sync). Computer-based MIDI sequencers (Atari ST running Notator/Cubase) driving multiple MIDI synths.

🎧 Classroom Slide 21 • Practical Production Task

House Music Workshop: Create a 16-Bar Track

As instructed in Slide 21 of the classroom presentation, apply your knowledge to construct an authentic 16-bar house track meeting every stylistic criterion.

The 16-Bar Production Checklist

  • ✓ A Programmed Drum Beat: 4-on-the-floor kick with offbeat open hi-hats (TR-909).
  • ✓ Swing Quantise: Apply 57%–62% shuffle swing to 16th-note percussion.
  • ✓ Vocal Sample: A chopped acapella hook phrase or soul/gospel loop.
  • ✓ Breakbeat or Rhythmic Samples: Layered acoustic funk fill or shaker.
  • ✓ Piano or Organ Riff: Syncopated chords or Korg M1 Organ 2 bassline.
  • ✓ TB-303 Riff or Stabs: Resonant low-pass filter modulation.
  • ✓ DJ Mix Intro & Breakdown: 8 bars of drums leading into a filtered breakdown.
  • ✓ Real-Time Filtering: Low-pass filter sweeps opening before the drop.
