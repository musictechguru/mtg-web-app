Mixing trainer

A 4-track mixer with inserts (high-pass filter, 3-band EQ, compressor), reverb and delay aux sends, pan and faders. Pick a challenge and work through the checklist. Each item ticks off when your mix meets it.

    Mixing tips

    Build the mix around the drums and bass, kept in the centre. Pan supporting parts to the sides to make room for the lead. High-pass anything that doesn't need low end. Sends are post-fader, so turning a track down also turns down its reverb and delay. Keep the low end dry: reverb on bass makes the mix muddy. Check the master meter never hits the red.

    The gate opens when the signal goes above the threshold. Attack is how fast it opens, hold keeps it open for a moment, and release is how fast it closes. A smaller range lets a little of the room sound through, which sounds more natural.

      Sampler trainer

      Learn how sampling works: trimming, loop points, zero crossings, reversing, transposing across the keyboard, bit depth and sample rate. Test yourself in Trainer, or get hands-on in Sampler.

      Drag the green start marker and red end marker. Gold shows the region that plays.

      The keyboard plays the region repitched: C3 is the original pitch. Each semitone up plays it about 6% faster, so it gets shorter as well as higher. Lower bit depths add quantisation noise; lower sample rates lose the top end and add aliasing.

      Loop challenge

      Make a perfect one-bar drum loop. At 100 BPM, one bar of 4/4 lasts 2.4 seconds.

        Delay trainer

        Work out delay times from the tempo, and learn to hear note-value delays. One beat = 60,000 ÷ BPM milliseconds. Switch to Calculator to check your working or set up a session.

        Tap 4 or more times
        One beat (1/4 note)

        Reverb starting points

        Pre-delay keeps the dry vocal clear before the reverb starts. Decay set to fade before the next beat or bar stops the reverb clouding the mix.
