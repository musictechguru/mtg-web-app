02 Technical Parameter & Circuit Architecture
Specification Parameter Engineering Description • Exam Definition
Delay Time Duration between dry signal and repeat (1ms to 2000ms+). Formula for dotted-eighth: (60,000 / BPM) x 0.75 ms.
Feedback (Regen) Percentage of delayed output returned to the input. 0% = single repeat; 100%+ = runaway analog self-oscillation.
High Damping Low-pass filter inside feedback loop attenuating treble on each repeat to simulate natural acoustic air absorption.
Tape Echo Flaws Mechanical wow and flutter motor speed variations that impart musical chorusing and harmonic tape saturation.
Signal Routing Auxiliary Send / Return bus setup (100% wet) allowing multiple channels to share one delay while preserving dry punch.

Question 1 [3 MARKS]

Explain how slapback delay was physically generated at Sun Studio in the 1950s without digital processors.

Examiner Mark Scheme • Guidance: Award 1 mark for mentioning reel-to-reel tape machine / magnetic tape. 1 mark for explaining the physical gap/distance between the record head and playback head. 1 mark for identifying tape transport speed (15 ips) creating a 75–130ms delay with no feedback.
Question 2 [3 MARKS]

Calculate the delay time in milliseconds required for a dotted-eighth note repeat in a track with a tempo of 120 BPM. Show your working.

Examiner Mark Scheme • Guidance: Award 1 mark for finding quarter note (60,000 / 120 = 500ms). 1 mark for finding eighth note (250ms) or applying 0.75 multiplier (500 x 0.75). 1 mark for correct final answer: 375 ms.
Question 3 [3 MARKS]

Describe the operational mechanism of an analog Bucket Brigade Device (BBD) delay chip and state one sonic limitation compared to digital delay.

Examiner Mark Scheme • Guidance: Award 1 mark for passing analog electrical charge packets sequentially across microscopic capacitor stages. 1 mark for clock oscillator controlling delay speed. 1 mark for limitation: clock noise / high-frequency roll-off / lower dynamic range / reduced headroom.
Question 4 [2 MARKS]

State two engineering advantages of routing a vocal delay as a parallel Auxiliary Send / Return rather than an inline Channel Insert.

Examiner Mark Scheme • Guidance: Award 1 mark each (max 2): (1) Preserves 100% of the dry vocal signal's clarity and transient punch; (2) Multiple tracks can be sent to a single shared delay processor, saving DSP/hardware; (3) Dedicated return channel allows independent EQ/automation of echoes.
Question 5 [2 MARKS]

Explain what occurs sonically and electrically when a delay unit's feedback parameter is increased past 100%.

Examiner Mark Scheme • Guidance: Award 1 mark for runaway self-oscillation (infinite repeats building in energy). 1 mark for circuit overload / non-linear analog harmonic distortion / saturated oscillation tone.
04 Section B: Extended Evaluation Essay (12 Marks)

Extended 12-Mark Technical Evaluation Question

Evaluate the evolution of delay technology from 1950s tape loops to modern digital and plugin processors. In your answer, refer to: • Mechanical, analog BBD, and digital architectures • Key parameters (delay time, feedback, high damping) • Signal routing (Inserts vs. Parallel Aux Sends) • Specific landmark musical examples.

Indicative Content • Examiner Commentary: • 1950s Tape Delay: Dual-deck slapback (Sun Records, Elvis) & dedicated tape loop units (Echoplex, Roland Space Echo). Mechanical motor speed governs time; wow/flutter creates organic pitch warmth; magnetic saturation provides soft clipping.
• 1970s Analog BBD: Solid-state capacitor bucket lines (Memory Man, Boss DM-2). Eliminates moving tape; darker repeats due to anti-aliasing low-pass filters.
• 1980s+ Digital Delay: RAM storage, PCM conversion, sample-accurate tempo sync (Korg SDD-3000, TC 2290). Dotted-eighth rhythmic doubling (The Edge / U2). Multi-tap, ping-pong, and dynamic ducking delay.
• Signal Routing: Parallel Aux Send/Return preserves dry transient impact and enables shared space vs. channel insert.
