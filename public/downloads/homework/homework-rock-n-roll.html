🔍 Task 2: The 'Big 4' Specification Fingerprints

Memorize these core specification criteria across Equipment, Capture, Processing, and Soundstage:

1. Equipment & Instruments

Fender Telecaster/Stratocaster, Gibson hollow-body electrics, Fender Bassman & Tweed valve amps, upright acoustic slap bass, acoustic drum kit with calfskin heads, Hammond B3 or upright piano.

2. Audio Capture & Micing

Minimal micing (1 to 3 mics total). RCA 44-BX/77-DX ribbons and Neumann U47 tube condensers. Distance micing with natural room spill. Vocals captured live with full rhythm section in single studio room.

3. Processing & Effects

Ampex 350/351 tape machine slapback delay (single repeat, 75-120ms created between record and playback heads). Valve preamp harmonic saturation. No digital EQ or compression; dynamic range controlled by performer distance.

4. Soundstage & Balance

Mono (single-track or 2-track mixed to mono). Acoustic balance determined by physical placement in room (brass/drums in rear, lead vocalist closest to mic). Center-focused depth created purely by room reflections.

📝 Task 3: Section A Exam Practice Questions (10 Marks)

Answer the following Edexcel exam questions in full technical sentences using professional studio terminology:

Question 1 [2 Marks]

State the primary technology used at Sun Studio to generate the signature slapback echo effect on Elvis Presley's vocal recordings.

Write your answer here (or complete on paper)...
Examiner Guidance: Mention reel-to-reel tape machine, delay between record and playback heads (or tape speed 15 ips, 75-120ms delay with no feedback).
Question 2 [2 Marks]

Explain why early 1950s Rock 'n' Roll drum tracks frequently exhibit substantial vocal and guitar spill.

Write your answer here (or complete on paper)...
Examiner Guidance: Live ensemble tracking in a single untreated or small studio room without isolation booths or acoustic baffles.
Question 3 [2 Marks]

Identify two distinct acoustic or electrical features of Chuck Berry's guitar tone on 'Johnny B. Goode'.

Write your answer here (or complete on paper)...
Examiner Guidance: Valve amplifier saturation/clipping, prominent midrange boost, bridge/middle pickup bite, natural tube compression.
Question 4 [4 Marks]

Describe the microphone transducer type predominantly favored for capturing brass and upright bass in 1950s sessions, and state one sonic benefit.

Write your answer here (or complete on paper)...
Examiner Guidance: Ribbon microphone (e.g. RCA 44/77). Smooth top end roll-off, fast transient response, figure-8 proximity effect boosting low fundamental warmth.
✍️ Task 4: Section B Extended 12-Mark Comparative Essay
Official 12-Mark Prompt

Compare the capture, processing, and sonic characteristics of Elvis Presley's 'Heartbreak Hotel' (1956) with Buddy Holly's 'Everyday' (1957). In your answer, refer to: • Instrumentation & Studio Capture • Signal Processing & Acoustic Effects • Stereophony, Panning & Balance.

Indicative Examiner Mark Scheme Content:

Elvis: Sun/RCA hallway chamber reverberation, slapback tape echo, live upright slap bass acoustic capture, valve tube distortion. Holly: Norman Petty close-miking innovation, knee-slap rhythm replacing standard drum kit, celeste capture with pristine high-frequency detail, dry intimate vocal placement.
