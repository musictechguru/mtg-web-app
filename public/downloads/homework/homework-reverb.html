02 Technical Parameter & Circuit Architecture
Specification Parameter Engineering Description • Exam Definition
RT60 Decay Time Time in seconds required for sound reflections to decay by 60 dB below the direct sound level.
Pre-Delay Gap Time offset (10–60ms) between direct sound and early reflections; preserves vocal intimacy before the wash.
EMT 140 Plate Suspended 1x2m cold-rolled steel sheet excited by transducer; dense, smooth, high-diffusion decay.
Spring Tank Coiled steel wires excited by electromagnetic transducers; twangy, dispersed, fluttery character.
Gated Reverb Chain Snare Drum → Massive Non-Linear Room/Plate → Aggressive VCA Compressor → Fast-Release Noise Gate.

Question 1 [2 MARKS]

State the precise acoustic definition of RT60.

Examiner Mark Scheme • Guidance: Award 1 mark for the time in seconds for reverberant sound reflections to decay. 1 mark for specifying a reduction of 60 dB below the direct/initial sound level.
Question 2 [3 MARKS]

Explain how Pre-Delay helps a lead vocal sit clearly in a modern mix without being masked by its reverb wash.

Examiner Mark Scheme • Guidance: Award 1 mark for explaining that pre-delay introduces a time gap (15–50ms) before reflections begin. 1 mark for stating that initial consonants/transients pass through completely dry and articulate. 1 mark for noting that the reverb blooms only after the transient, preventing masking.
Question 3 [3 MARKS]

Describe the mechanical construction and operation of an EMT 140 plate reverb.

Examiner Mark Scheme • Guidance: Award 1 mark for a 1x2 meter cold-rolled steel sheet suspended under tension inside a heavy frame. 1 mark for an electromagnetic driver/transducer vibrating the plate with audio. 1 mark for contact pickups capturing the bending waves / mechanical damper pad controlling decay time.
Question 4 [3 MARKS]

Detail the complete signal chain and gate settings used to create the 1980s gated snare drum sound.

Examiner Mark Scheme • Guidance: Award 1 mark for feeding the snare to a large room or plate reverb. 1 mark for heavy compression (e.g. SSL listen mic compressor). 1 mark for noise gate set with threshold open on the snare hit, hold time ~150-250ms, and instantaneous fast release (10-20ms) cutting off the tail abruptly.
Question 5 [2 MARKS]

Explain how Convolution Reverb captures and recreates the acoustic properties of a physical concert hall.

Examiner Mark Scheme • Guidance: Award 1 mark for recording an Impulse Response (IR) of the room using a starter pistol or sine sweep. 1 mark for mathematically multiplying (convolving) the input audio signal with the IR using digital signal processing.
04 Section B: Extended Evaluation Essay (12 Marks)

Extended 12-Mark Technical Evaluation Question

Evaluate the development and musical application of artificial reverberation technologies: • Acoustic chambers and EMT plate reverbs • Spring reverb tanks in guitar amplifiers • Digital algorithmic (Lexicon) and convolution processors • The role of pre-delay, damping, and gated reverb in production.

Indicative Content • Examiner Commentary: • Physical Chambers: Tiled basement rooms with speaker/mic (Putnam, Capitol, Abbey Road). Natural, smooth 3D decay but fixed acoustic characteristics.
• EMT 140 Plate (1957): Suspended 1x2m steel sheet; eliminates room construction; dense high-frequency diffusion ideal for vocals; adjustable mechanical damper.
• Spring Reverb: Coiled steel springs (Fender Twin). Frequency-dependent dispersion creates iconic surf 'twang' and mechanical flutter.
• Digital Algorithmic (Lexicon 224/480L): Microprocessor feedback delay networks; user controls RT60, room size, pre-delay, and damping.
• 1980s Gated Reverb: Townhouse/SSL Phil Collins technique; non-linear acoustic impact without muddying fast tempos.
• Convolution: Mathematical impulse response reproduction of real acoustic spaces.
