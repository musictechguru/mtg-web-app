Question 1: Comparative Transduction (Fender Rhodes vs. Wurlitzer 200A)
[4 Marks]
Compare the mechanical striking and electrical pickup transduction mechanisms of the Fender Rhodes and the Wurlitzer 200A electric pianos. Explain how these design differences affect their respective acoustic timbre and sustain.
Specification Examiner Guidance:
  • Fender Rhodes: Neoprene hammer strikes a steel tine coupled to a tuned tonebar (asymmetric tuning fork). Transduced via an individual electromagnetic pickup (coil around a permanent magnet) through electromagnetic induction. Yields bell-like attack, high sustain, and mellow fundamental.
  • Wurlitzer 200A: Felt hammer strikes a tuned steel reed. Transduced electrostatically via a capacitive pickup plate charged with ~170V DC polarising voltage. Yields a sharper, reed-like bite, quicker decay, and barking mid-range when driven hard.
Question 2: Hammond B3 Tonewheels & The Leslie 122 Rotary Cabinet
[6 Marks]
(a) Explain how the Hammond B3 tonewheel generator produces pitch, referencing tooth counts, motor gearing, and electromagnetic induction. [3 Marks]

(b) Describe how the Leslie 122 rotating speaker cabinet produces frequency modulation (Doppler effect) and amplitude modulation, noting the two operational speeds. [3 Marks]
Specification Examiner Guidance:
  • Tonewheel Generator: 91 notched/scalloped steel discs rotate adjacent to permanent magnetic pickups with wire coils. Tooth rate across pole piece induces an alternating electric current whose frequency matches the musical pitch. Controlled via 9 harmonic drawbars (additive synthesis).
  • Leslie 122 Mechanics: Two-way system with a rotating upper horn (treble) and rotating downward-firing bass baffle drum (low frequencies). Rotate in opposite directions.
  • Acoustic Effects: Dual speeds: Chorale (slow ~40 RPM) and Tremolo (fast ~400 RPM). Sound source moving toward and away from ears/microphones produces the Doppler pitch shift (FM) combined with directional amplitude sweep (AM).
Question 3: Extended Analysis • The Mellotron M400 & Pre-Digital Sampling
[10 Marks]
"The Mellotron M400 is considered the direct electro-mechanical forerunner to the modern digital sampler, yet its physical tape playback mechanism imposed radical sonic and performance limitations on musicians."

Evaluate this statement. In your answer, explain:
  • The mechanical operation of individual magnetic tape strips, pinch rollers, capstan drive shaft, and playback heads.
  • The physical constraint of the 8-second tape run and spring return system.
  • The acoustic character of tape wow, flutter, tape saturation, and mechanical keyboard touch.
  • Reference at least one historically significant 1960s or 1970s recording (e.g., The Beatles, King Crimson, Genesis).
Examiner Level 3 Descriptor Guidance:
  • Tape Mechanics: Depressing a key engages a pinch roller that forces 3/8-inch magnetic tape against a constantly spinning capstan shaft directly over an audio replay head. Tape is pulled from a serpentine storage frame.
  • 8-Second Limit: Each tape strip is exactly 8 seconds long and non-looping. When released, an internal return spring pulls the tape back to the start. Impossible to sustain indefinitely without re-striking; forced players into detached, rhythmic, or arpeggiated phrasing.
  • Authentic Timbre: Wow, flutter, frequency roll-off, tape compression/saturation, and distinct natural instrumental attack transients (e.g., flutes, violins, cellos) recorded at Abbey Road / IBC.
  • Case Study Example: Strawberry Fields Forever (Beatles / 1967) - John Lennon / Paul McCartney playing the famous Mellotron Mark II flute intro with characteristic tape flutter and attack transient.
