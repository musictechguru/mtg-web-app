02 Technical Parameter & Circuit Architecture
Specification Parameter Engineering Description • Exam Definition
All-Pass Filters Active RC filter stages that pass all frequencies at equal gain while shifting the phase angle of specific bands.
Non-Harmonic Notches Phase cancellation creates logarithmic, unevenly spaced notches, giving a warm, smooth, vocal-like chew.
Stage-to-Notch Ratio Every pair of all-pass stages produces 1 notch. 4-stage = 2 notches; 8-stage = 4 notches; 12-stage = 6 notches.
Feedback / Resonance Recirculating phased output deepens notches and sharpens peaks, adding an intense whistling resonance.
Pre vs. Post Distortion Placed before distortion produces warm, dynamic, amp-compressed chew; placed after distortion creates harsh, slicing sweep.
03 Section A: Specification Exam Questions (13 Marks)

Answer all questions in the spaces provided. Complete mark schemes and examiner guidance are included for self-assessment and peer marking.

Question 1 [3 MARKS]

Explain how an analog phaser creates phase cancellation notches without utilizing an audio time delay line.

Examiner Mark Scheme • Guidance: Award 1 mark for active all-pass filter stages (operational amplifiers with RC networks). 1 mark for shifting the phase angle of specific frequencies up to 180°. 1 mark for summing the phase-shifted signal with the direct dry signal, creating destructive phase cancellation at out-of-phase frequencies.
Question 2 [2 MARKS]

State the key spectral difference between the cancellation notches of a Phaser and the comb filter notches of a Flanger.

Examiner Mark Scheme • Guidance: Award 1 mark for phaser notches being spaced non-harmonically (logarithmically/non-linearly). 1 mark for flanger notches being spaced in a linear harmonic series.
Question 3 [2 MARKS]

How many cancellation notches are generated by an 8-stage phaser like the MXR Phase 100?

Examiner Mark Scheme • Guidance: Award 1 mark for stating 4 notches. 1 mark for explaining that each pair of all-pass filter stages generates exactly one cancellation notch.
Question 4 [3 MARKS]

Why were matched JFET transistors critical in the analog circuitry of pedals like the MXR Phase 90?

Examiner Mark Scheme • Guidance: Award 1 mark for JFETs acting as voltage-controlled variable resistors in each all-pass stage. 1 mark for LFO voltage modulating resistance to sweep notch frequencies. 1 mark for explaining that unmatched JFETs cause filter stages to sweep unevenly, ruining the smooth phase swirl.
Question 5 [3 MARKS]

Describe the sonic difference between placing a phaser BEFORE an overdriven amplifier versus placing it AFTER the amplifier in an effects loop.

Examiner Mark Scheme • Guidance: Award 1 mark for recognizing signal chain placement. 1 mark for before overdrive: warm, subtle, dynamic, chewy tone as the amplifier's tube compression smooths the sweep. 1 mark for after overdrive: bright, dramatic, aggressive sweep filtering the clipped harmonics.
04 Section B: Extended Evaluation Essay (12 Marks)

Extended 12-Mark Technical Evaluation Question

Evaluate the design, engineering principles, and production applications of the phaser effect. In your answer, refer to: • All-pass filter architecture and phase cancellation • The role of the LFO and feedback controls • Comparison with flanging and wah-wah filtering • Signal routing and landmark guitar recordings (Eddie Van Halen / David Gilmour).

Indicative Content • Examiner Commentary: • Circuit Engineering: All-pass operational amplifiers shift phase without time delay; each 2 stages yield 1 notch (4-stage Phase 90 = 2 notches; 8-stage Phase 100 = 4 notches). JFETs modulate resistance via LFO.
• Spectral Characteristics: Non-harmonic notch spacing produces warm, organic vocal qualities unlike linear comb filtering.
• Feedback / Resonance: Recirculating phased signal narrows resonant peaks, adding intensity.
• Comparative Analysis: Wah-Wah sweeps a single resonant band-pass boost; Phaser sweeps multiple cancellation troughs across full spectrum; Flanger uses true time delay.
• Landmark Tones: Eddie Van Halen's 'Brown Sound' (Phase 90 before Plexi); David Gilmour's Pink Floyd clean arpeggios ('Shine On You Crazy Diamond').
